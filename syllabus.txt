Course description
How sustainable is the fiscal deficit? Why is the US richer than Rwanda? These are a couple of questions that we deal with in macroeconomics.  Macroeconomics studies the behavior and performance of the overall economy. It looks at the big questions, namely growth, unemployment, and inflation in the short and in the long run.

In this course you will learn the main concepts in macroeconomics through relevant case studies. Through connections with 'real world' examples, you will acquire macroeconomic vocabulary and concepts and you will understand basic macroeconomic mechanisms.

We are living  interesting macroeconomic times. Throughout the course we will look at current and historical macroeconomic issues in the US and in the world.

Queens College General Education Statement: This course satisfies the Individual and Society (IS) requirement of the Pathways General Education Flexible Core.

Information
This class meets on Tuesdays and Wednesdays from 9:15 to 10:45 noon at Remsen 300.
Credit hours: 3
Course requirements: High school algebra
Expected time commitment: Students should devote at least 5 hours per week to watch, study, and revise the contents of each unit covered.
Course and learning objectives: The main goal of this course is for you to learn how to think, talk, and write as a macroeconomist. To achieve such ambitious goal, we will focus on the main problems in macroeconomics and build the connections to understand and explain the macroeconomy.
Objectives
As your instructor, I expect you to:

achieve a higher level of economic literacy;
learn the basic macroeconomic concepts;
understand the economic forces at play in the economy;
examine the effects of different macroeconomic policies;
connect the macro theory with suitable real-world examples;
apply the concepts learned in an accurate and relevant fashion;
further develop your analytical and critical thinking skills throughout the variety of activities and assignments;
show your engagement in the topics covered in class through active participation and analysis.

Communication
Check your e-mail! Today, the preferred method of communication is e-mail. Hence, I assume you check your QC e-mail regularly.
When should you expect a reply from yours truly? I check my e-mail regularly so you can expect an answer from me within 48 business hours.  I will not answer e-mails regarding a particular assignment the day before it is due. (Tip: start early.) I don't answer to e-mails outside business hours or during weekends.
How should you write an e-mail?
When e-mailing me, always always include your course AND section number (not the time the class meets or just the course number).  I teach multiple sections of the same course so it is time consuming for me to figure out in which section you are actually enrolled. Moving forward, if you want a reply from me, include that information, otherwise I won’t be able to help you. I receive hundreds of e-mails, please include an informative subject line. If you reply to the last announcement I have sent, it would not be moved to my priority e-mail inbox.
You should compose your e-mails following the appropriate e-mail etiquette. I am happy to help; however, I do not appreciate blank e-mails with attachments without a proper salutation and relevant information about the nature of your e-mail. I receive hundreds of e-mails per day and I cannot tend to them unless I know what the e-mail is about. This may not be your fault, you may not know that this is expected from you. If you don’t know about how to e-mail a professor, here is a guide. It is important for you to learn how to address e-mails professionally, not just to your professors but with any other person you engage in a professional manner. Writing your e-mail in ALL CAPS is read as you shouting. Please don't do that. We are not texting, we are not friends. My time is valuable, so is yours. By abiding to these guidelines you will, most likely, get the answers you seek. Please always type an informative subject, your course and section number, and your name.
You must e-mail me from your QC e-mail account. Make sure you check the e-mail address you have registered on QC. If not, you won't receive e-mail announcements. I cannot change your e-mail address in the system. You have to do that yourself!
One of the most useful skills to gain in college is to be resourceful. I ask you to consult the syllabus, resources online, your notes, and your classmates before you ask me a question via e-mail. In general, most professors (including myself) do not like to answer emails about logistical issues (how something will be graded, how to access a file, when something is due, etc.) that are readily available. It would take you roughly the same amount of time it would take them to write and wait for a reply to an email.
What are office hours? Office hours are for clarifying concepts and solving specific problems. To ensure that we make the best use of each other's time, come prepared to office hours, know exactly what your needs are.I have scheduled office hours over Zoom every week. Make sure you have specific questions on the topic you need help with. 
What if  you have questions about your grade for an assignment? If you have doubts about the grade you earned in any of the assignments, please come to office hours or contact me within 10 days after the grade was posted.

Your final grade for this course is the result of your performance in a variety of assignments throughout the semester: participation (5%), weekly quizzes  (at least 12)  ( 10%),  4 tests (81%, 20.25% each), 8 problem sets (4%, 0.5 percentage point each). On average, students are expected to devote at least 5 hours per week to succeed in this class. You will have an hour  for tests. There will be extra credit throughout the semester, embedded in assignments and tests. Your final grade for the course will be curved according to the department's guidelines. 

Participation
Your performance in this class depends on your engagement in class and through the textbook and platform.  Each unit --in the reading section-- has embedded questions to check your understanding of the material. I will also have polls in class to check your understanding. By answering all the questions you will get full points for participating, whether you get them right or wrong! The idea is that you read and work through the questions at your own pace. The questions are designed to make you assimilate and understand the main concepts that we study better. 

You also have access to videos and slides where I explain some of the  main concepts in more depth and answer questions from students. 

Quizzes
There will be at least one quiz each week, covering material from the previous week. Quizzes will account for 10% of your final grade, and your two lowest quiz grades will be dropped. Under a new department policy adopted in response to the growing use of AI tools, introductory courses are required to include regular quizzes. The quizzes for this course will be administered through the Top Hat platform.

Problem sets
There will be eight problem sets due a few days after a given unit takes place. All the deadlines are on the syllabus, and on each problem set --located within each unit's folder.  You earn points by answering the questions. Answer keys will be available a few days after the problem set is due. I won't accept problem sets after the due date as the answer key will be available. Please note that the problem sets will switch from "Homework" to "Review" mode after the deadline. This means that after the deadline you can still look at the problem set but you won't be able to submit answers. You can collaborate with others for the problem set but you must submit your own problem set. All submissions are through the portal.

Tests
There are four tests, three of them will take place during regular lecture schedule and the fourth one during finals week. I will drop the lowest score. (At the end of the semester, I will reweigh the tests to amount to the full 81%.) If you can't make it to the test for whatever reason, you'll get this "freebie." No questions asked!  If you decide not to take any given test, it will count as zero. Tests and assignments will be graded promptly, I won't "pre-grade" your assignments, that is I won't check your work before the assignment is due.
